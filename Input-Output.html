Input & Output Devices

Interacting with the Digital World

1. Direct Data Entry (Barcodes & Scanners)

Barcode Scanners (2D and 3D)

When a barcode is scanned, a red laser or LED light is reflected back. The dark bars absorb light, while white spaces reflect it. A photoelectric cell detects the light intensity and converts it into binary data.

QR Codes (Quick Response)

Unlike 1D barcodes, QR codes are 2D and can hold significantly more data (up to 7,000 digits). They are read by cameras/imaging devices using three large squares in the corners for alignment.

Optical Character & Mark Recognition

  • OCR: Converts scanned text into editable digital files.
  • OMR: Detects marks on a specific position on a paper (e.g., multiple-choice exams).
2. Touchscreens: Capacitive vs. Resistive

Capacitive

Uses a glass layer coated with a conductive material. When a finger touches the screen, the electrostatic field is changed. Sensors at the corners calculate the coordinates.

Pros: Multi-touch support, very durable. Cons: Requires bare skin or conductive stylus.

Resistive

Consists of two flexible layers with a gap. Pressing down makes the layers touch, completing a circuit. The change in resistance is measured.

Pros: Use with gloves/any object, cheaper. Cons: No multi-touch, easily scratched.

3. Sensors in Action

Sensors are the primary input for Control and Monitoring systems. They send continuous Analogue signals which must be converted to Digital via an ADC (Analogue-to-Digital Converter).

Common Sensors and Applications:

  • Temperature: Used in Central Heating (Central-Heat-Sys.png) and Greenhouses (Green-House.png).
  • Acoustic/Sound: Detecting breaking glass in security systems.
  • Moisture/Humidity: Managing soil levels in agriculture.
  • PIR (Infrared): Detecting motion for automated doors or lights.
4. Output: Projectors and Actuators

Digital Light Processing (DLP)

Uses millions of Micro-mirrors on a tiny chip. These mirrors tilt toward or away from the light source thousands of times per second. A color wheel spins to create the full spectrum of light.

Actuators

An actuator is an electromechanical output device. It takes the digital command from the computer and converts it into physical movement (opening a valve, starting a motor, or moving a robotic arm).
